Three principles, nothing else

Every decision in this project, from architecture to a single line of UI copy, resolves to one of these.

  1. 01

    Free and open source, without compromise

    The full source is open under MIT, forever. No feature is held back for a paid tier, no network calls of any kind, no account required. Being open source isn't a marketing label — it's the reason the other two principles are possible.

  2. 02

    Standing on the shoulders of giants

    Wikilinks, YAML frontmatter, a folder of plain files. Where a good convention already exists, this project adopts it instead of inventing a competing one, so your existing notes and habits carry over with as little friction as possible.

  3. 03

    A user's notes belong to the user

    Notes are plain markdown files in a folder you control, not a database or a proprietary format. Nothing about how a note is stored depends on this application continuing to exist, being installed, or being online.

Built for how you already take notes

Point it at any local folder of markdown files. No import step, no proprietary format, no setup wizard. Your notes are the app, not the other way around.

  • Wikilinks and backlinks — [[links]] resolved by filename, autocomplete while typing, backlinks panel.
  • Graph view — a pannable, zoomable map of how every note connects.
  • CodeMirror 6 editor — source mode, rendered preview, or split view. Your call, per note.
  • Fully offline, no account — no telemetry, no network calls, no sign-in. Ever.
  • Frontmatter and kanban — YAML properties, case-insensitive boards, smart collections.
  • Task Hub — every GFM task-list item across your vault in one place.
  • Local speech-to-text — whisper.cpp on desktop, SpeechRecognizer on Android. Nothing leaves your device.
  • Full-text search — across the entire vault, including hidden dotfiles.
